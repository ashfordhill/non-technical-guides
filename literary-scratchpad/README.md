## Self Doubt Illusions

> Mike White picked up the phone in his hotel room in Honolulu a few days before the 1983 Hula Bowl. Bill Walsh was on the other end, and White could tell something was wrong. Walsh told White he was ready to quit as the 49ers' coach, and he wanted White to take over the team.
>
> White sat in stunned disbelief as he listened to his best friend tell him he was done.
>
> Only a year earlier, Walsh had been carried off the field at the Pontiac Silverdome after winning the Super Bowl. He had taken the 49ers from the bottom of the NFL in 1979 to a world championship after the 1981 season, and now he was ready to step down as coach?
>
> White was both astonished and confused. But as the two men spoke, White understood Walsh's angst and realized what was happening.
>
> Walsh's world had again begun to unravel, and the self-doubt he had frequently experienced during his long and difficult journey to the top of the NFL had begun to creep in once more. The reason for the latest bout of uncertainty rested with the team's failure to make the playoffs the year after winning it all.
>
> — *Guts and Genius: The Story of Three Unlikely Coaches Who Came to Dominate the NFL in the '80s*

  - Bill Walsh takes an underdog team to the win a Super Bowl after incremental progress
  - Next season his team doesn't make the playoffs and he considers quitting coaching
  - Ends up being one of greatest NFL coaches of all time, altering the game itself (West Coast Offense)
  
When reading about Walsh's achievements, it's surprising to learn just how many ups & downs, swinging moods and long periods of despair he experienced along the way. This is not unique to Bill Walsh; many of us have the capacity to reach mental states that are self-defeating and not congruent with reality. I definitely relate to this.

## Dopamine is a Helluva Drug

Certain genes cause some people to be more dopamine-seeking than other. 

> People who have a long form of the DRD4 gene, such as the 7R allele, are more likely to take risks. They pursue new experiences because they have a low tolerance for boredom. They like to explore new places, ideas, foods, drugs, and sexual opportunities. Worldwide about one in five people have the 7R allele, but there's substantial variation from place to place."
>
> Once it was established that the 7R allele of the DRD4 gene was related to how far a population migrated, the next question was why? The obvious answer is that dopamine makes people seek out more. It makes them restless and diassatisfied."
>
> Many American immigrants came from Europe, a migration that boosted the dopaminergic gene pool in the United States, leaving Europe with a residual population more likely to take a [less dopamine-driven] approach to life.
>
> — *The Molecule of More: How a Single Chemical in Your Brain Drives Love, Sex and Creativity - and Will Determine the Fate of the Human Race*

Dopamine-seeking tendendies can be great for adventures, innovation, driving change and so much more.

At the same time, it's important to remember that true contentment and fulfillment come from the present; in other words we must slow down. As with everything, balance is key. I find this balance challenging, as someone who craves large amounts of dopamine and stability at the same time. There's a nagging fear that if I'm not out chasing, achieving, making progress in something, then I'll have squandered my finite lifespan somehow. Zooming out a bit, it's kind of amusing to think this way; I've found that positive feelings from achievements are **very** fleeting, while human (and animal!) relationships, a sense of belonging and stopping to smell the roses are what brings me lasting joy.
